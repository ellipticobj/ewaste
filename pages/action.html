Used tech does not mean bad tech

A few months ago, I needed a new phone. Instead of buying a brand new iPhone 17 Pro, I decided to look for a certified refurbished model. I was skeptical at first, wondering if it would last and if the battery would hold up?

What I found was an iPhone 16 pro that not only worked perfectly, looked flawless, cost a third of its original price, and kept one more piece of e-waste out of the landfill. The experience changed how I think about buying used. Every device we choose to keep in use rather than replace is a small win for the planet.

Now I always choose to buy used phones, laptops, and accessories whenever possible. It takes a little more research, but the savings and the peace of mind are worth it to me.

Buying & Selling Used Tech Safely

A practical guide to protecting your data and your wallet.

01

Wipe Your Data Completely

Before selling or trading in any device, perform a factory reset. On computers, use a secure erase tool or overwrite the drive. On phones, remove SIM and SD cards, sign out of all accounts (iCloud, Google), then factory reset. For SSDs, use the manufacturer's secure erase utility to ensure data is unrecoverable.

02

Check Battery Health

Battery condition is the #1 concern for used electronics buyers. On iPhones, go to Settings > Battery > Battery Health. On Android, use an app like AccuBattery. On MacBooks, check System Information > Power. A battery above 80% capacity is considered healthy. Factor replacement cost into your offer.

03

Research Before You Buy

Stick to reputable platforms with buyer protection (Carousell, eBay, Back Market). Read seller reviews, check return policies, and verify IMEI/ serial numbers. Ask for photos of the actual device, not stock images. Look for listings that mention "battery health" and "no cosmetic damage". Remember, detailed sellers are trustworthy sellers.

04

Know What to Inspect

When you receive a used device, check the screen for dead pixels, test all ports and buttons, verify Wi-Fi and Bluetooth work, run a camera test, and check for signs of water damage (liquid contact indicators). Most platforms give you a short return window, don't be afraid to use it. If you're unsure of what to check for, AI chatbots are just a tap away.
